\begin{abstract}
Rate-splitting multiple access (RSMA) promises spectral gains in the
\emph{overloaded} multi-user MIMO uplink, where the number of users $K$ exceeds
the receive antennas $N_r$, but harvesting those gains requires jointly choosing
transmit power, the common/private stream split, and the
successive-interference-cancellation (SIC) decoding order under imperfect channel
state information (CSI) and hard per-user quality-of-service (QoS) limits. We
formulate energy-efficiency (EE) maximization under \emph{per-SNR-point}
delivery-ratio and queue-stability constraints relative to a strong SDMA
reference---constraints that must hold \emph{simultaneously at every operating
point}, not on average---and show that this requirement defeats both classical
baselines and single-timescale deep reinforcement learning (DRL). We propose a
two-timescale hierarchical policy: a slow meta-controller that sets user grouping
and group-level stream split, and a fast self-attention graph worker that proposes
per-slot power shares, queue drain ratios, and a Plackett-Luce SIC order. The
worker is trained with a delivery-gated Lyapunov composite reward (DGLCR)
combining a Dinkelbach-linearized EE term, max-weight throughput, and CVaR-tail
delivery-virtual-queue penalties, under a Lagrangian delivery constraint with
lexicographic gradient projection. Because the reward landscape is
non-differentiable through the combinatorial SIC order, we introduce a
\emph{solver expert}: a differentiable shadow of the MMSE-SIC rate engine that
refines power logits online and supplies demonstrations; the policy is distilled
by behavior cloning and fine-tuned with a stepwise-frozen protocol (SFTP) with
guard-selected checkpoints. On a 7-point SNR grid (0--30\,dB, imperfect CSI, 50
episodes/point), the policy clears all 7 per-point gates with mean delivery
$0.9496 \pm 0.0047$ across five seed replications and dominates SDMA, NOMA and
equal-power baselines on delivery, queue and overflow at every SNR---at roughly
half their transmit power (30\,dB: 0.888 vs.\ 0.664/0.581/0.601). We further show
that the solver expert \emph{itself}, run as a training-free per-slot optimizer, is
the strongest controller on this grid (mean delivery 0.9574), establishing a
model-based upper bound; distillation compresses it into a single-forward-pass
policy (0.9441) and the frozen fine-tune recovers $\approx$59\% of the compression
gap (0.9519), so the learned policy's value is inference cost and model-free
deployment rather than absolute performance. A pre-registered fine-tuning campaign
further shows that released-learning-rate, trust-region, anchoring, and
data-reshaping variants all fall within the policy's own seed dispersion,
motivating multi-seed confidence-interval comparison as the honest reporting
standard for this class of methods. Finally, a Shapley-value-based
interpretability analysis (global feature-subset selection via the SVEA
classification game, plus expected-gradient attributions validated against a
model-agnostic KernelSHAP proxy under multiple reference constructions) shows
that the learned scheduler's power decisions are driven foremost by queue
backlog and channel gain, with the queue block as the dominant feature across
operating points.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cell-edge and machine-type traffic increasingly force base stations to serve more
active users than they have receive antennas. In this \emph{overloaded regime}
($K > N_r$) spatial multiplexing alone saturates: interference, not noise, bounds
the sum rate, and queueing delays grow without a scheduling policy that is
explicitly queue-aware. Rate-splitting multiple access (RSMA) mitigates overload
by splitting each message into a \emph{common} stream decoded by all users and a
\emph{private} stream decoded by its intended user, with successive interference
cancellation (MMSE-SIC) at the receiver
~\cite{yu2022robust,karim2025uplink,clerckx2016rate}.
Realizing RSMA's promise, however, requires solving a coupled, per-slot
combinatorial problem---power allocation across users, the common/private split,
and the SIC decoding order---repeatedly and online, under imperfect CSI and a
realistic power-consumption model.

Three properties make this problem resistant to existing approaches.
\emph{(1) Hard per-point constraints:} practical deployments specify QoS at
discrete operating points (SNR levels), e.g.\ a minimum delivery ratio and a
maximum queue length \emph{at each point}, benchmarked against a deployed baseline
such as SDMA; a policy excellent on average but violating one point is
unacceptable. \emph{(2) Imperfect CSI in the overloaded regime:} pilot-based
estimation injects noise whose variance scales as $\sigma^2/(K p_{\rm pilot})$ per
user, so overload worsens estimation quality exactly where spatial resources are
scarcest; robust designs bound the residual error
$\|\Delta \vect{h}_k\| \le \varepsilon$ but the interaction with SIC error
propagation makes closed-form robust allocation brittle. \emph{(3) Energy
efficiency without loopholes:} maximizing $\mathrm{EE}=R_{\rm sum}/P_{\rm consume}$
under $P_{\rm consume}=\sum_k P_k/\eta_{\rm PA}+P_{\rm circ}$ removes the
degenerate ``transmit nothing'' optimum but couples the fractional objective to
the queueing state, requiring fractional-programming treatment (Dinkelbach) inside
a stochastic control loop.

Deep reinforcement learning is a natural candidate for (1)--(3), yet naive DRL
fails for a structural reason: the combinatorial SIC order and non-differentiable
rate expressions block gradient-based credit assignment, and the reward's delivery
gate creates sparse, saturating gradients at exactly the points that matter.
Recent robust and distributionally-robust DRL beamforming
~\cite{schroder2025modelfree,wang2024distributionally,cui2025drsac} and
generalization analyses~\cite{demirel2025generalization,luan2025robust}
address robustness to CSI perturbation but not the simultaneous per-point QoS
structure. Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty methods
~\cite{zhu2025lyapguid,neely2010stochastic,zakeri2024lyaplearn} provide queue-stability
guarantees for \emph{given} rate regions but do not learn the combinatorial
allocation itself; policy-distillation practice in RANs~\cite{khosravi2025practical}
motivates our teacher$\to$student design but does not couple distillation to
per-slot combinatorial refinement under hard per-point gates.

Related lines of work span: expert-driven imitation and expert iteration
\cite{pomerleau1989alvinn,ross2011dagger,rusu2016policydistill,anthony2017exit,silver2016alphago};
deep-RL foundations for continuous control and discrete optimisers
\cite{williams1992reinforce,mnih2015dqn,schulman2015trpo,haarnoja2018sac,hasselt2016doubleq,sutton2018book};
queue-stability and cross-layer scheduling theory
\cite{tassiulas1992stability,georgiadis2006tutorial};
RSMA and NOMA rate-region analysis
\cite{mao2018rsma,clerckx2016rate,clerckx2021openoma};
deep-learning and deep-RL resource allocation in wireless systems
\cite{liang2019v2v,luong2019drlcomm,zhang2019mlwireless};
and fractional-programming tools for communication EE
\cite{dinkelbach1967,shen2018fractional}.

\emph{Contributions.} (i) We formalize EE maximization for overloaded RSMA
MU-MIMO uplink under per-SNR-point delivery and queue constraints relative to
SDMA, with a lexicographic criterion isolating the binding constraints.
(ii) A two-timescale hierarchical policy: slow meta-controller (grouping +
group-level split) and fast self-attention graph worker (power shares, drain
ratios, Plackett-Luce SIC order) with state-augmented observations.
(iii) A delivery-gated Lyapunov composite reward (DGLCR) with Dinkelbach
linearization, max-weight throughput and CVaR-tail delivery-virtual-queue
penalties, under a Lagrangian delivery constraint with lexicographic gradient
projection. (iv) Solver-expert distillation: a differentiable shadow of the
MMSE-SIC engine (float64-consistent to $10^{-9}$) that refines power logits
online, supplies demonstrations that alone clear all 7 gates, and is distilled
into the policy; a stepwise-frozen fine-tune protocol (SFTP) with guard-selected
checkpoints preserves and extends the distilled behavior. (v) An honest empirical
account: full-grid comparison against SDMA/NOMA/equal-power; seed replication
quantifying intrinsic dispersion ($\sigma_{\rm seed}\approx 0.005$); and a
pre-registered fine-tuning campaign showing five mechanism variants fall within
that dispersion. (vi) A Shapley-value-based interpretability analysis
(\S\ref{sec:interp}): global feature-subset selection via the SVEA
classification game and expected-gradient attributions validated against a
model-agnostic proxy under multiple references, showing that the learned power
decision is driven by queue backlog and channel gain while the drain, decoding
order and meta-grouping heads collapse to near-constant defaults.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Network and RSMA transmission}
A single base station with $N_r$ antennas serves $K$ users, each with $N_t$
transmit antennas, over $T$ slots per episode. User $k$'s message is split into a
common part (share governed by $\alpha_k \in [\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]$) and a
private part. Users assigned to group $g$ share a common stream decoded by all
group members; all common streams precede all private streams in the decoding
order, and common-stream SIC cancellation is performed only within a group. The
transmitted signal of user $k$ is
\begin{equation}
x_k = \sqrt{\alpha_k p_k}\, s_k^c + \sqrt{(1-\alpha_k) p_k}\, s_k^p,\qquad
\mathbb{E}|s_k^c|^2 = \mathbb{E}|s_k^p|^2 = 1,
\label{eq:tx}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_k=0$ recovers unsplit (SDMA/NOMA-type) transmission. The BS applies
unified MRT transmit weights
$\vect{W}_c,\vect{W}_p \in \mathbb{C}^{N_r\times K}$ under per-user caps
$P_k \le P_{k,\max}$. The receiver performs MMSE-SIC in decoding order $\pi$. For
a stream decoded at position $j$ in $\pi$, the rate is $R=\log_2(1+\mathrm{SINR})$
with SINR denominator collecting (i) interference from not-yet-decoded streams,
(ii) SIC residual from decoded streams scaled by hardware imperfection
$\beta^2$, and (iii) CSI-error residual scaled by $\varepsilon^2 p$, plus thermal
noise $\sigma^2$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{(j)} &= \frac{p_k\,|\vect{w}^{\mathsf H}\hat{\vect{h}}_k|^2}
{I_{\rm undec} + \beta^2 I_{\rm dec} + \varepsilon^2 P_{\rm tot}\|\vect{w}\|^2 + \sigma^2\|\vect{w}\|^2},\\
I_{\rm undec} &= \!\!\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{D}_j}\! p_i\,|\vect{w}^{\mathsf H}\hat{\vect{h}}_i|^2,\qquad
I_{\rm dec} = \!\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_j}\! p_i\,|\vect{w}^{\mathsf H}\hat{\vect{h}}_i|^2,
\end{split}
\label{eq:sinr}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}_j$ is the set decoded before position $j$. The MMSE combiner is
regularized by the worst-case bound via $\varepsilon^2 P_{\rm tot}\vect{I}$
(robust MMSE). Evaluating \eqref{eq:sinr} at the common- and private-stream
positions of user $k$ gives $\mathrm{SINR}_k^c,\mathrm{SINR}_k^p$, and the
per-user and sum rates follow as
\begin{multline}
R_k = \log_2\!\big(1+\mathrm{SINR}_k^c\big) + \log_2\!\big(1+\mathrm{SINR}_k^p\big),\\
R_{\rm sum} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} R_k.
\label{eq:rates}
\end{multline}
Declared rates use $(\hat{\vect{H}},\varepsilon)$; actual rates use
$(\vect{H},\varepsilon{=}0)$; the outage-aware rate is their minimum, so
over-declared rates are penalized by real throughput.

\subsection{Imperfect CSI and channel dynamics}
Channels follow a 3GPP UMi path-loss model (users at 50--300\,m, exponent 3.76,
normalized at 100\,m) with AR(1) temporal correlation
\begin{align}
\vect{h}_k[t] &= \rho\, \vect{h}_k[t-1] + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\Delta\vect{h}_k[t],\\
\Delta\vect{h}_k[t] &\sim \mathcal{CN}\!\big(\vect{0},\,\sigma_h^2\vect{I}\big), \quad \rho=0.9,
\label{eq:ar1}
\end{align}
i.e.\ slow Rayleigh fading whose coherence grows with $\rho$. Pilot-based MMSE
estimation yields per-user error variance
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\rm est}^2 = \frac{\sigma^2}{K\,p_{\rm pilot}} + \varepsilon_{\rm floor}^2,
\label{eq:csi}
\end{equation}
so estimation quality degrades with load $K$; the residual is bounded
$\|\Delta\vect{h}_k\|\le\varepsilon$. Training randomizes noise power over
$\sigma^2 \in [-5,35]$\,dB per episode; evaluation is at fixed points under
imperfect CSI.

\subsection{Traffic, queues and QoS classes}
Arrivals $A_k[t]$ are Poisson with SNR-adaptive mean
$\lambda(\mathrm{SNR})=\mathrm{load}(\mathrm{SNR})\,C(\mathrm{SNR})/K$,
$\mathrm{load}(\cdot)$ a sigmoid from 0.75 to 1.3; three QoS classes scale
arrival/drift/overflow weights. Queues evolve as
\begin{equation}
Q_k[t{+}1] = \min\{\max\{Q_k[t]-D_k[t],0\}+A_k[t],\, Q_{\max}\},
\label{eq:queue}
\end{equation}
with served bits
\begin{equation}
D_k[t] = \min\{Q_k[t],\ d_k[t]\, R_k[t]\},\qquad d_k[t]\in[0,1],
\label{eq:served}
\end{equation}
where $d_k$ is the worker's queue-drain ratio, and overflow $O_k[t]$ counting
bits dropped at $Q_{\max}$. The delivery ratio over an episode is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{deliv} = \frac{\sum_k \sum_t D_k[t]}{\sum_k \sum_t A_k[t]},
\label{eq:deliv}
\end{equation}
the fraction of arrived bits served within the horizon.

\subsection{Power consumption and energy efficiency}
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm consume} = \frac{\sum_k P_k}{\eta_{\rm PA}} + P_{\rm circ},\quad
\mathrm{EE} = \frac{R_{\rm sum}}{P_{\rm consume}},
\label{eq:ee}
\end{equation}
with $\eta_{\rm PA}=0.35$, $P_{\rm circ}=5.0$\,W and a floor
$P_{\rm eff}=\max(P_{\rm consume},P_{\rm floor})$ removing the degenerate
$P\to0$ optimum.

\subsection{Problem formulation and evaluation criterion}
Let $\mathcal{S}=\{s_1,\dots,s_7\}$ be the evaluation SNR grid (0--30\,dB). The
constraint set, relative to a reference SDMA policy, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\mathrm{deliv}_\pi(s) \ge \mathrm{deliv}_{\rm SDMA}(s),\\
&\mathrm{queue}_\pi(s) \le 1.1\,\mathrm{queue}_{\rm SDMA}(s),
\end{split}
\label{eq:gates}
\end{equation}
for all $s\in\mathcal{S}$ (a \emph{relative two-bar gate} at each point). The
reported score is the lexicographic tuple
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score} = \big[\ \#\{s:\text{gate met}\},\ \overline{\mathrm{deliv}},\ -\overline{\mathrm{queue}},\ \mathrm{EE\ ratio}\ \big],
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
so cleared points dominate first, then mean delivery, queue, and EE ratio. A
global aggregate check gates termination but does not substitute
\eqref{eq:gates}. Collecting the per-slot decisions into
$\vect{x}[t]=(\vect{g},\vect{\alpha},\vect{p},\vect{d},\pi)[t]$, the design
problem is the constrained stochastic control problem
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\max_{\{\vect{x}[t]\}}\ \liminf_{T\to\infty}\ \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{EE}[t]\\
&\text{s.t.}\quad \eqref{eq:gates},\ \ p_k[t]\le P_{k,\max},\ \ \bar{Q}_k<\infty,\ \ \pi[t]\in\mathcal{S}_K,
\end{split}
\label{eq:p1}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_K$ denotes the valid SIC permutations. Because a single
policy must satisfy \eqref{eq:gates} at \emph{every} point simultaneously,
\eqref{eq:p1} cannot be decomposed per SNR point, which is precisely the
structure that defeats per-point-agnostic baselines (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Proposed Framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Two-timescale MDP}
The slow scale (every $T_s=20$ slots) selects grouping $g_k\in\{0,\dots,G-1\}$ and
group-level split $\vect{\alpha}\in[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]^G$ expanded per
user. The fast scale (every slot) selects the $4K$-dim action: power-share logits
(softmax shares $\times K$, clipped), drain ratio $d_k\in[0,1]$, and
Plackett-Luce scores $z_k$~\cite{plackett1975,luce1959} inducing SIC order $\pi$ by
Gumbel top-$K$ sampling:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&P(\pi\mid\vect{z}) = \prod_{i=1}^{K}\frac{z_{\pi(i)}}{\sum_{j=i}^{K} z_{\pi(j)}},\\
&\pi = \arg\mathrm{sort}\big(-(\log\vect{z}+\vect{\gamma})\big),\quad
\gamma_i\sim\mathrm{Gumbel}(0,1),
\end{split}
\label{eq:pl}
\end{equation}
with $\log P(\pi\mid\vect{z})=\sum_i\big[\log z_{\pi(i)}-\log\sum_{j\ge i}
z_{\pi(j)}\big]$ supplying the differentiable permutation log-probability for
the policy gradient; at deployment the order is deterministic,
$\pi=\arg\mathrm{sort}(-\vect{z})$. Power logits are mapped to shares by a
clipped softmax over users, so the $4K$ action stays in a bounded domain.
Observations are per-user feature blocks plus globals; the worker observation is
$13K{+}3$ dimensional --- per-user channel gain, queue, queue change, arrivals,
power and rate history, and, via equal-dimension state augmentation of the
interference/fairness/distance blocks, the Lagrangian-bucket, delivery-debt (DVQ)
and Dinkelbach-ratio blocks, plus split $\alpha$, group id and QoS load/priority
fields, and global time, SNR and CSI-error scalars --- and a history window of
the last $H$ slots augments each user token. This is the feature-block space on
which the interpretability analysis of \S\ref{sec:interp} operates.

\subsection{Hierarchical policy architecture}
The meta-controller embeds per-user meta-features, applies multi-head
self-attention, and outputs categorical group logits (temperature annealing
$\tau:2.0\to0.5$, balance regularizer), a group-level Gaussian head mapped by a
sigmoid to $[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]$, and a mean-pooled critic. The worker
is a self-attention graph network (SA-GAT): per-user tokens FiLM-modulated by
global context with SNR and CSI-error scalars injected per token; 2 layers of
4-head attention (hidden 128) feed per-user heads for the $4K$ action and a
mean-field critic pooling QoS class statistics.

\subsection{Delivery-gated Lyapunov composite reward (DGLCR)}
With served bits $D_k$, effective power $P_{\rm eff}$, Dinkelbach ratio
$q^\star$, delivery-debt queue $Y$ and power-budget queue $X$:
\begin{multline}
r = c\Big[ V\big(\textstyle\sum_k D_k - q^\star P_{\rm eff}\big)
+ w_{\rm tp}\textstyle\sum_k \tfrac{Q_k}{Q_{\max}} w^{\rm qos}_k D_k\\
+ w_y \tfrac{Y}{Y_{\rm ref}} \textstyle\sum_k D_k
- w_q \textstyle\sum_k \tfrac{Q_k^2}{Q_{\max}^2}\\
- \textstyle\sum_k w^{\rm qos}_k O_k
- w_x \tfrac{X}{X_{\rm ref}} P_{\rm tot} \Big],
\label{eq:dglcr}
\end{multline}
where the first term is the Dinkelbach-linearized EE~\cite{dinkelbach1967}
($q^\star$ on an outer loop),
the second max-weight throughput, the third delivery-debt coupling, and the rest
queue/overflow and power-budget penalties. Sigmoid gates on the EE term and on
penalties prevent the low-power trap $\partial\mathrm{EE}/\partial P<0$ from
dominating while delivery is infeasible. The Dinkelbach ratio is refreshed on an
outer loop as
\begin{equation}
q^\star \leftarrow \frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_k D_k\big]}{\mathbb{E}\big[P_{\rm eff}\big]},
\label{eq:dinkelbach}
\end{equation}
which is exactly the linearization that turns the fractional objective
$\mathrm{EE}=R_{\rm sum}/P_{\rm eff}$ into the first term of \eqref{eq:dglcr}.

\subsection{Constraint stack}
A Lagrangian dual variable $\lambda$ on the delivery constraint ($\ge0.98$) is
updated with warmup and cap; its per-step signal is an EMA of instantaneous
delivery. The EE policy-gradient is projected onto the null space of the
delivery-constraint gradient (lexicographic projection). Per-user delivery
virtual queues (DVQ) with CVaR tail focus (worst 25\%) concentrate penalty on
users risking the per-point gate. Concretely, with $c\ge0$ the delivery-constraint
residual and $\vect{g}$ the EE policy-gradient,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\lambda \leftarrow \big[\lambda + \eta_\lambda(\tau - \widehat{\mathrm{deliv}})\big]_+^{\lambda_\cap},\\
&\vect{g} \leftarrow \vect{g} - \frac{\vect{g}^{\sf T}\nabla c}{\|\nabla c\|^2}\,\nabla c
\quad \text{if } c\ge 0,\ \vect{g}^{\sf T}\nabla c < 0,
\end{split}
\label{eq:lagproj}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the dual ascent is warm-started and capped, and the projection removes the
component of the EE gradient that would push the policy back into the
delivery-infeasible region.

\subsection{Solver expert and differentiable rate engine}
Because $R(\cdot)$ is non-differentiable through $\pi$ and the min-outage
operator, we implement a torch shadow of the MMSE-SIC rate computation, verified
float64-consistent with the immutable reference to atol $10^{-9}$. Per slot, the
expert runs a short Adam refinement (25 steps, lr 0.1, cold start) on power logits
against a DGLCR surrogate mirroring the active reward branch, plus
candidate-selection among decoding orders (gain-descending / queue-last).
Formally, for each candidate order $\pi_o$ the expert solves
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\theta^{(i+1)}_{\pi_o} = \theta^{(i)}_{\pi_o} - \eta_{\rm Adam}\,
\nabla_\theta \hat{J}_{\rm DGLCR}(\theta;\pi_o),\quad i=1,\dots,25,\\
&o^\star = \arg\max_o \hat{J}_{\rm DGLCR}(\theta^{(25)}_{\pi_o};\pi_o),
\end{split}
\label{eq:expert}
\end{equation}
cold-started every slot, where $\hat{J}_{\rm DGLCR}$ is the differentiable
surrogate mirroring the active reward branch and $\theta$ are the power logits.
Equation \eqref{eq:expert} contains no learned policy: it is a training-free,
model-based per-slot optimizer whose only ingredient is the differentiable rate
engine. The expert exceeds SDMA delivery at every tested SNR (0/5/15/30\,dB:
$+4.0/+1.4/+10.0/+23.9$ percentage points) at lower power.

\subsection{Distillation and stepwise-frozen fine-tuning (SFTP)}
Demonstrations from the expert (8{,}000 episodes) train the policy by behavior
cloning (BC) on continuous heads and the Plackett-Luce head; BC alone clears all 7
gates. The policy is fine-tuned with PPO under SFTP: an anchor term injecting the
BC gradient into the lexicographic-projection path, critic-only warm-up, a
persistent anchor with slow decay, and guard-based checkpoint selection.
Behavior cloning minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm BC} = \mathbb{E}\big[\|\vect{a}^{\rm cont}_\theta -
\vect{a}^{\rm cont}_{\rm expert}\|^2\big]
- \mathbb{E}\big[\log P(\pi_{\rm expert}\mid\vect{z}_\theta)\big],
\label{eq:bc}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ mean-square error on the continuous heads (power shares, drain ratios)
plus the Plackett-Luce negative log-likelihood of the expert's decoding order,
and the fine-tune optimizes the clipped surrogate
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm PPO} = \mathbb{E}\big[\min\big(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t,\
\mathrm{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t\big)\big]\\
- c_v \mathcal{L}_{\rm VF} + c_e \mathcal{H}[\pi_\theta],
\end{split}
\label{eq:ppo}
\end{equation}
with importance ratio $r_t(\theta)=\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)/
\pi_{\theta_{\rm old}}(a_t|s_t)$, GAE advantage $\hat{A}_t$, value loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\rm VF}$ and entropy bonus $\mathcal{H}$.
The combination of KL early-stopping and the learning-rate decay floor collapses the
actor learning rate to its floor early ($\sim$update 100 of $\sim$780), an
\emph{emergent stepwise freeze} acting as an implicit trust region around the
distilled basin; \S\ref{sec:results}-D examines whether this freeze is causal.

\subsection{Training protocol}
Policy-gradient optimization via PPO~\cite{zhang2023pporsma,sutton1999policy} with
clip 0.1, rollout 2048, batch 64, 8 epochs, GAE $\lambda=0.95$, actor lr
$3\times10^{-5}$; a difficulty-annealing curriculum (5 stages) and hard-example
mining re-weighting per-SNR-bucket sampling by delivery shortfall with streak
multipliers and performance-linked entropy incentives. All mechanism flags
default off so the official evaluation profile is unchanged. The complete
pipeline is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:train}.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Two-timescale hierarchical training with solver-expert distillation (SFTP)}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Run the solver expert \eqref{eq:expert} for $8{,}000$ episodes; store $(s_t,\ \vect{a}^{\rm cont}_{\rm exp},\ \pi_{\rm exp})$
\State Pre-train worker and meta policies by BC on $\mathcal{L}_{\rm BC}$ \eqref{eq:bc}
\State Initialize $\lambda$, $q^\star$, delivery-debt queue $Y$, power-budget queue $X$
\For{episode $=1,\dots,N$}
  \State Meta: sample $(\vect{g},\vect{\alpha})$; hold fixed for $T_s$ slots
  \For{slot $t=1,\dots,T$}
    \State Worker: sample $(\vect{p},\vect{d},\pi)$ from $\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t)$ via \eqref{eq:pl}
    \State Env: rates \eqref{eq:rates}, queues \eqref{eq:queue}, reward \eqref{eq:dglcr}
  \EndFor
  \State Update $q^\star$ \eqref{eq:dinkelbach}; update $\lambda$ and project gradients \eqref{eq:lagproj}
  \State PPO update on $\mathcal{L}_{\rm PPO}$ \eqref{eq:ppo} with SFTP anchor and critic warm-up
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint by the per-point guard (6 SNR points $\times$ 16 episodes + queue bar)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\emph{Environment.} $K=24$, $N_r=16$, $N_t=2$, $T=200$, $P_{k,\max}=1$\,W (24\,W
aggregate), $Q_{\max}=100$ bits; 3GPP UMi path loss; AR(1) $\rho=0.9$;
SNR-dependent CSI noise with $\varepsilon_{\rm floor}=0.01$; unified MRT +
MMSE-SIC; adaptive arrivals; 3 QoS classes.

\emph{Evaluation.} Official sweep over $\mathcal{S}=\{0,5,10,15,20,25,30\}$\,dB, 50
episodes/point, imperfect CSI; gates and lexicographic score per
\S\ref{sec:system}. Every score is produced by the same immutable evaluation
script; checkpoints selected by the guard are re-evaluated officially.

\emph{Comparator set (classical + recent).} Beyond classical SDMA
~\cite{he2023eemumimo}, NOMA~\cite{wang2023nomaee} and equal-power allocation
~\cite{falloun2025fixedpa}, we compare against four recent (2025--2026)
algorithms re-implemented in the identical environment and protocol: (i) \emph{LDPTRLQ-style} single-timescale
Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty RL in the family of Lyapunov-guided DRL for energy
efficiency~\cite{zhu2025lyapguid} (queue-stable DPP reward, no hierarchy, no
distillation); (ii) \emph{DR-SAC}~\cite{cui2025drsac},
distributionally-robust SAC trained against the worst case over the SNR/CSI
uncertainty domain (ICLR 2026); (iii) \emph{neural-DPP}-style learned per-slot
drift-plus-penalty control in the family of learning-augmented Lyapunov
scheduling~\cite{zakeri2024lyaplearn}; (iv)
\emph{DiffOpt}, a training-free model-based comparator running the differentiable
MMSE-SIC shadow engine as a per-slot online optimizer (the solver expert without
any learned policy), isolating learning from per-slot optimization. The
repository's adaptive-power and water-filling heuristics are additional
non-learned references. Trained comparators (i)--(iii) share the observation/action
interface and are pre-registered before training; DiffOpt/adaptive/water-filling
are evaluated on the identical grid (\texttt{scripts/evaluate\_extended\_baselines.py}).

\emph{Replication and pre-registration.} The champion configuration is replicated
verbatim at seeds 43 and 44 (besides seed 42). All fine-tuning variants were
pre-registered (hypothesis, arms, single-variable controls, accept/reject gates)
before launch; verdicts were written without post-hoc gate changes. Seed
dispersion is reported alongside every comparison.

\subsection{Seed replication and the intrinsic noise floor}
Verbatim replication at seeds 43--46 yields best-guard deliveries
$\{0.9503, 0.9414, 0.9519, 0.9526\}$; with the champion (0.9519) the five draws
give mean $0.9496$, sd $0.0047$, range $0.0112$ (the pre-registered downgrade
trigger, range $>0.012$, is not met). The seed-44 best-guard ($0.9414$) is a
checkpoint-selection artifact of the legacy 3-point mini-eval (its own final is
$0.9491$); seed 46's draw (0.9526) is statistically equivalent to the champion
($\Delta0.0007$). End-of-training snapshots are markedly less stable than
guard-selected checkpoints (finals n=4: mean 0.9362, sd 0.0200, vs best-guard sd
0.0047), retrospectively validating guard-based selection as the delivery
artifact. Hence (i) the recipe centers at $0.9496\pm0.0047$ and the champion is
one draw from that band, reproducible within $\pm0.002$; (ii) any comparison with
margin $\lesssim0.005$ is below seed resolution at single-seed sample sizes.

This re-frames the contribution hierarchy honestly: the differentiable engine plus

steps with an explicit rate model), not absolute performance. Earlier ``RL
surpasses the teacher'' wording is withdrawn: RL surpasses the \emph{distilled
student} (BC), not the live optimizer, which had not previously been evaluated on
the official grid.

\emph{Trained recent-family comparator.} A single-timescale Lyapunov
drift-plus-penalty RL baseline in the LDPTRLQ family~\cite{zhu2025lyapguid} (PPO
with queue-Lyapunov penalties and the same curriculum/mining protocol, but no
hierarchy, no distillation, no guard) clears \textbf{0/7 gates} (mean delivery
0.782, mean power 23.2\,W $\approx$ saturated; Fig.~\ref{fig:band}). Vanilla
queue-Lyapunov RL therefore cannot solve this overloaded instance from scratch;
together with bc\_init (7/7 from pure distillation) this localizes the enabling
ingredient to \emph{expert initialization}, not to the reward family or the
hierarchy alone.

\emph{Classic continuous-control family (TD3/DDPG/SAC).} The review-standard
TD3/DDPG pair plus SAC, adapted to the mixed PL+continuous action space
(deterministic pathwise actor through the differentiable $\tanh(\mu)$ continuous
head and greedy PL order; twin-Q TD targets with target-policy smoothing and a
delayed actor for TD3, single-Q for DDPG; single-timescale, no distillation, no
guard): TD3 clears 0/7 gates (mean delivery 0.818 at a saturated 24\,W transmit
power), SAC also 0/7 (mean 0.787, its per-point queues exceed the gate at every
SNR), and DDPG reaches 5/7 (mean 0.886) but still misses the two low-SNR gates
$\{0,5\}$\,dB on both delivery and queue. All fall below the distilled policy
(bc\_init 7/7, 0.9441) and confirm the LDPTRLQ pattern: from-scratch RL cannot
solve this overloaded instance, whatever the algorithm family.

\emph{Optimization-depth check (JointOpt).} A per-slot optimizer with 60
warm-started Adam steps (vs.\ DiffOpt's 25 cold-start) scores below DiffOpt at
every point (mean 0.913 vs.\ 0.957, $\approx$13\,W): the model-based upper bound
is not step- or warm-start-limited---cold-start 25-step DiffOpt is already tight,
consistent with the earlier warm-vs-cold diagnostics.

\emph{Information-set vs.\ computation budget.} The policy's deficit against
DiffOpt could stem from (i) the oracle (true-channel) information in the solver's
surrogate or (ii) the per-slot optimization budget. Table~\ref{tab:classic}
separates both axes: the causal variant (surrogate restricted to the estimated
channel $\hat{\vect{H}}$) reproduces the oracle variant point-for-point at 50
episodes ($\max|\Delta|\le 0.001$), and the deeper 60-step warm-started JointOpt
scores \emph{below} the 25-step cold-start DiffOpt (0.913 vs.\ 0.957). The gap to
the oracle is therefore intrinsic to replacing per-slot online optimization with
a single shared forward pass---it is neither an information-set nor a step-budget
artifact.

\subsection{Checkpoint selection matters}
The seed-44 episode exposes an evaluation pathology: a legacy 3-point mini-eval
selected a checkpoint 0.9\,pt below the same run's final model, because its
saturated points cannot see mid-band regressions the official 7-point
$\times$50-episode sweep reveals. A queue-aware 6-point guard (v2) catches these
(v1 would have passed 5 bad checkpoints on the M5.2-D1 arm). Checkpoint selection
must use the same per-point gate structure as the final evaluation.

\subsection{Statistical assessment}
Per-point deliveries are paired across the 7-SNR design grid (df\,$=$\,6;
two-sided paired $t$-test with Cohen's $d$; Holm--Bonferroni correction at
$\alpha=0.05$ across the eight external-family comparisons). The champion
recipe's five-seed best-guard mean is $0.9496\pm0.0047$ (95\% CI
$[0.9438,0.9555]$). \textbf{Ours} significantly exceeds SDMA ($p\,{=}\,0.018$,
$d\,{=}\,1.22$), equal-power ($p\,{=}\,0.004$, $d\,{=}\,1.69$), water-filling and
adaptive-power ($p\,{=}\,0.016$/0.023, $d\,{=}\,1.26$/1.15) and the from-scratch
RL arms (LDPTRLQ/TD3/DDPG, $p\le0.006$, $d\ge1.6$); NOMA is non-significant
after correction ($p\,{=}\,0.015$ raw, $d\,{=}\,1.28$). Against our engine's
\emph{oracle} per-slot optimizer the difference is small but detectable
($p\,{=}\,0.045$, $d\,{=}\,-0.96$): the single-pass causal policy sits a
$\approx$0.6\% compression gap below the oracle---the quantified cost of
per-slot-online-optimization-free inference. Baselines are single-seed (seed
42); our recipe's multi-seed dispersion is reported in the seed-replication
subsection.

\subsection{Interpretability: what the policy attends to}
\label{sec:interp}

Beyond aggregate delivery/EE numbers, we ask which \emph{state features} drive the
learned worker and meta policies, following two Shapley-value-based frameworks.
\textbf{Global feature-subset selection via SVEA}~\cite{tripathi2020svea}: we treat
the 13 per-user observation blocks as players of a classification game whose
characteristic function is the reduction in hinge-loss training error of a linear
ERM classifier that predicts whether the policy serves a user in the current slot
($v(S) = \tilde{c} - \operatorname{tr\_er}(S)$, $\tilde{c}$ the intercept-only error).
The Shapley error-apportioning $e_j = \tilde{c}/n - \phi_j$ admits a universal
threshold of zero: features with $e_j < 0$ (the \emph{dominant} set
$\mathrm{SVEA}_{neg}$) are the ones whose knowledge alone reproduces the service
decision. Fig.~\ref{fig:svea} reports $e_j$ with 95\% t-intervals per SNR point: the
queue block is the sole dominant feature at 0--15\,dB, split-$\alpha$ joins it at
20\,dB, and no feature dominates at 30\,dB (the policy serves near-uniformly and
the balanced game finds no discriminative signal) --- queue is the only feature
whose dominance is consistent across points, overlapping the hand-selected
mean-field critic channels but derived automatically (Table~\ref{tab:svea}).
The power of classification $PSV(\mathrm{SVEA}_{neg})$ (subset/full-set test
accuracy) stays $\geq 0.95$, i.e.\ the dominant features nearly reproduce the full
13-feature service model. The arrivals/$\lambda$-bucket/Dinkelbach-$\eta$ blocks
are constant within each fixed-SNR evaluation point and are excluded as null
players (their marginal contribution is identically zero).
\textbf{Local attribution and reference sensitivity}~\cite{fernando2019deepshap}:
for fixed states we compute expected-gradient attributions (model-introspective
backpropagation against a reference) for the four decision objects --- total/per-user
power share, drain ratio, decoding-order confidence, and meta group/$\alpha$ ---
under four reference constructions (all-zero, per-block marginal means, empirical
bootstrap, semantically neutral), and validate against the model-agnostic KernelSHAP
proxy via top-5 recall and reference-pair Jaccard overlap
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overlap} and Table~\ref{tab:recall}). Mirroring the finding of
~\cite{fernando2019deepshap}, attributions are visibly reference-sensitive
(reference-pair top-5 Jaccard often below 0.5), and the bootstrap/marginal-means
references agree best with the model-agnostic proxy on the power target
(recall@5 $0.8$--$1.0$ across SNR) while the all-zero reference is the least
reliable --- motivating the bootstrap reference as the paper's reporting default.
KernelSHAP shows queue-backlog and channel-gain blocks as the dominant drivers of
the total-power decision, consistent with the DGLCR design intent. Worker $\alpha$
is excluded as a dead dimension (overridden by the meta split mask).

The remaining three decision objects are \emph{near-state-invariant} in the
distilled policy: attributions for them are effectively zero, and direct rollout
statistics confirm the mechanism rather than an artifact of the attribution.
The drain ratio saturates at $d_k \equiv 1$ at every point ($\mathrm{std} < 10^{-4}$
across all 16{,}800 slots), the decoding order's top choice repeats only
$\approx$0.15--0.19 of the time (vs.\ $1/24$ uniform), i.e.\ the softmax
confidence on the top user is feature-flat even though the order is weakly
structured, and the meta controller places essentially all users in a single
group ($\approx$1--2 groups per slot) with a near-constant split
$\alpha \approx 0.15$--0.20 whose variance grows only at 30\,dB. The power
allocation is therefore the \emph{only} strongly state-driven learned decision;
the drain/order/grouping heads sit at learned default values, indicating that
the SFTP distillation compressed the optimizer's power behavior into the policy
and the other action heads kept their initialization-scale defaults --- a
concrete simplification opportunity for the architecture (fixed drain/order,
or dropping the meta grouping).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Why does the freeze help?} The emergent early learning-rate collapse acts as
an implicit trust region around the distilled basin, preventing drift into regions
where the non-differentiable outage/SIC structure makes the surrogate reward
misleadingly optimistic (notably 25--30\,dB). Released-lr variants drift exactly
there. Yet \S\ref{sec:results}-D shows the frozen-vs-released difference is within
seed noise, so the freeze is a stabilizer of a seed-robust operating point, not a
proven causal lever; the multi-seed response surface is the correct instrument.

\emph{The optimizer upper bound and what learning buys.} The training-free
per-slot optimizer (DiffOpt) exceeding the learned policy at every point forces an
honest account: greedy model-based optimization with an accurate differentiable
rate model is a stronger controller than any policy we trained, and the
distillation+fine-tune pipeline is best understood as \emph{compressing that
optimizer into a fast, model-free policy} at a quantified cost
(0.9574 $\to$ 0.9519, $\approx$1.2\% mean delivery) in exchange for
single-forward-pass inference and no runtime dependence on the rate model. The
causal (estimate-only) variant retains the full advantage at 50 episodes, so the
upper bound holds for fair causal controllers; the remaining open question is
whether the distillation gap can be closed by distilling the optimizer's
queue-conditioned behavior rather than its marginal actions.

\emph{Statistical honesty as a contribution.} Single-seed RL comparisons at effect
sizes $\sim0.005$ are indistinguishable from seed noise here. Pre-registered
campaigns and explicit noise-floor quantification are intended as a template:
report seed dispersion, pre-register gates, treat within-band differences as null.

\emph{Limitations.} Fixed $K=24,N_r=16$; CPU-costly official evaluation limits
replication; the expert surrogate inherits the reward's shaping choices; the
emergent freeze is configuration-dependent and must be re-verified under reward or
curriculum changes. The interpretability analysis~\S\ref{sec:interp} is
single-checkpoint and single-seed: the SVEA game uses a linear-surrogate ERM on
the ``served'' outcome label, and the features constant within a fixed-SNR point
(arrivals, Lagrangian-bucket, Dinkelbach ratio) carry no FSS information by
construction; local attributions are reported under an empirical-bootstrap
reference, chosen because it agrees best with the model-agnostic proxy, but the
reference choice itself remains a judgment call, as documented
in~\cite{fernando2019deepshap}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a hierarchical reinforcement-learning framework with solver-expert
distillation for energy-efficient scheduling of overloaded RSMA MU-MIMO uplink
under per-SNR delivery and queue constraints. The distilled, stepwise-frozen
policy clears all seven evaluation gates with seed-robust mean delivery
$\approx0.950\pm0.005$ and dominates classical baselines across the SNR range at
substantially lower transmit power. A pre-registered fine-tuning campaign
establishes the seed-dispersion floor and sets multi-seed confidence-interval
comparison as the reporting standard. Future work includes the multi-seed
freeze-onset response surface and extending the solver expert to jointly refine
the stream split, plus extending the interpretability analysis
(\S\ref{sec:interp}) to per-user, per-slot attributions and to the queue-growth
outcome label.
